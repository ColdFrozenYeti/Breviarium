Privacy Policy

Effective 11 October 2026

Breviarium collects no data about you.

The app

Breviarium works entirely offline. It makes no network connections of any kind: there is no account, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting and no tracking. Every text is inside the app, and the office is computed on your iPhone.

The only thing the app keeps is your settings (the rite, the office, the theme, the font, the text size and so on), which iOS stores on your iPhone for the app's own use. They never leave the device, except inside the backups of your iPhone that you make yourself, which Apple's own terms cover. Deleting the app deletes them.

Apple

Breviarium is downloaded and updated through the App Store, which Apple runs under its own privacy policy. If you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple may show me anonymous, aggregated figures, such as the number of downloads or crashes. They never identify you, and I cannot see who you are.

If you write to me

If you email me, I use your address and your message only to reply and to fix what you report. I do not share them with anyone, and I will delete them if you ask.

This website

These pages are served by GitHub Pages. They use no cookies, no analytics and nothing loaded from elsewhere, though GitHub may keep its own logs of visits for security; see GitHub's privacy statement.

Children

Since the app collects nothing from anyone, it collects nothing from children.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date. The app's source code is public, so anyone can check what it does.
