Breviarium 1.6.1 fixes three things reported from TestFlight, and turns the psalms'
alternation round.

**Fixes**

- **Tapping a reminder closed the app** (1.6). The notification centre's delegate used
  the `async` form of `didReceive`, and its compiler-made thunk handed UIKit its
  completion from a background thread, which UIKit refuses
  (`_updateSnapshotAndStateRestorationWithAction`). It now uses the completion-handler
  forms and calls them on the main thread (`NotificationRouter.swift`).
- **The Kalendarium opened at January, then cut to the day shown** (1.4). It scrolled
  from `onAppear` after the sheet had appeared. It still scrolls there (twice, the second
  pass correcting the lazy list's estimate), but the list now stays hidden until it has,
  and its rows are identified by their own ID. (iOS 18's initial `ScrollPosition` was
  tried first and overshot to December on the iPad.) The UI tests check, on the iPhone
  and the iPad, that 19 November is on screen as soon as the sheet is up.
- **At Compline, the Glória Patri after Deus in adiutórium had its italics the wrong way
  round** (1.4): the 1.4 rule (*Glória Patri* upright, *Sicut erat* and *Allelúia* in
  italic) applied only in an Introductio section, and at Compline *Deus in adiutórium*
  closes the short lesson's. It now applies wherever *Glória Patri* answers a versicle
  and response (`ContentBlock.swift`).

**Changed**

- **A psalm's first verse is upright** (the user, 15 November 2026): its verses go
  upright, italic, upright, and so on, where until 1.6 the first was italic. The same goes
  for the canticles and for the English column of a psalm paired whole (Pius XII
  psalter). The psalm's *Glória Patri* carries the alternation on, so after a psalm with
  an odd number of verses it is italic and *Sicut erat* upright. The *Glória Patri*
  answering *Deus in adiutórium* stays upright, with *Sicut erat* and *Allelúia* in italic.
  The Theme and Font galleries' sample already started upright.
