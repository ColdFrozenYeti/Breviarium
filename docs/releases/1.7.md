Breviarium 1.7 is built for the **iPhone Duo**, adds a tenth app icon, **Thea**, and a
**Keep screen awake** setting, and shows **Northumberland Inverted** on a Dark Home Screen
(`docs/1.7_plan.md`).

**What's new**

- **The iPhone Duo**, natively. Built with the iOS 27 SDK, the app fills the Duo's whole
  open screen, rather than sitting in a box in the middle of it, and moves with it as it
  folds and unfolds. Folded, it is the iPhone app as before. The page keeps clear of the
  vertical status bar down the open screen's right edge. Two pages side by side need a
  window at least 900 pt wide and wider than tall, as on the iPad; the Duo's open screen
  is narrower than that in the simulator, so there it shows one page.
- **Thea**, a tenth app icon, last in the gallery: the initial B of the *Psalter of Hanuš
  from Kolovrat* (National Library of the Czech Republic, Osek 71), redrawn in pink. Its
  source image is CC BY-SA 4.0, so the icon is shared under that licence; the credit is on
  the About screen and in `NOTICE.md`.
- **Northumberland Inverted on a Dark Home Screen**: with the Home Screen set to *Dark*,
  Northumberland Simple (the default icon) and Northumberland show Northumberland
  Inverted. The other icons look the same in light and dark.
- **Keep screen awake**, under *Reading* in Settings, off by default. On, the screen never
  dims or locks while Breviarium is open; leave the app and the phone locks as usual.

**Fixes**

- **Reminders could wait for good on the notification centre** (1.6). Each change cleared
  every reminder, without waiting for the clearing, before adding the wanted ones, and
  waited on every call to the notification centre in turn; one call that never answered
  held up every later change. Only the reminders no longer wanted are removed now (adding
  one under an identifier already scheduled replaces it), and every call to the
  notification centre has five seconds to answer before the app carries on. This was
  found on the iOS 27.2 simulator, whose notification service never answers (below); it
  hasn't been seen on a phone.

**Behind it**

- **The iOS 27 SDK** (Xcode 27.2, on GitHub's `xcode-27` runner image), which the Duo needs
  to run the app natively. The minimum stays **iOS 26.0**: every iPhone and iPad that ran
  1.6 runs 1.7, and anything that needs a newer iOS is simply absent on an older one
  (decided 2026-11-17).
- **App CI** runs its UI tests on iOS 27.2 simulators: the iPhone 17, the iPad Air 11"
  (M4, where the iOS 27.2 runtime has no M3) and the **iPhone Duo**, in five jobs
  rebalanced on the new simulators' times, the second device of a job booting while its
  iPhone classes run. A check confirms both dark icons are in the built app.
- `DuoUITests`: upright and on its side, English off and on, vertical scrolling,
  *Menus: Hidden*, the Kalendarium, Settings and Notifications. The tests follow the
  window's size: a spread only where it is at least 900 pt wide.
- **The real notification centre's test skips itself on the iOS 27.2 simulator** (decided
  2026-11-17): there the permission alert appears, but no call to the notification centre
  ever returns, on the iPhone and the iPad alike. The test reads the app's debug trace and
  skips only when its calls time out, so it runs again once a simulator's service answers.
  The other eight reminder tests, through the stand-in, run as before; a real reminder is
  checked on the phone from TestFlight.
- The widget gallery reads the window's screen rather than `UIScreen.main` and the device
  idiom, as Apple asks for the Duo.
- `Keep screen awake` is one UIKit call, `isIdleTimerDisabled`, in `ScreenAwake.swift`,
  set at launch, on every change and whenever the app comes back to the front. It needs no
  permission or entitlement, so the sideloaded build has it too.
- The website (support FAQ and privacy policy) describes 1.7: the iPhone Duo, the widgets,
  the reminders, *Keep screen awake* and ten icons.
