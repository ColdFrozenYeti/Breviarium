# 1.4: answering the 4.2 rejection

**Approved 12 November 2026.** App Review rejected 1.3 (103) under Guideline 4.2, Minimum
Functionality, on 28 October 2026, reviewing it on an iPad Air 11" (M3). This plan is
the answer: the user resubmits once 1.4 builds.

## Context

Apple rejected Breviarium 1.3 (103) under **Guideline 4.2, Minimum Functionality**, on 28 Oct 2026. They reviewed it on an **iPad Air 11" (M3)**. This is the second round. The first was a 2.1 *Information Needed* request, which the user answered in full.

**Diagnosis.** To a reviewer the app looks like an ebook. Because it is iPhone-only, the iPad ran it in a small compatibility window. Inside it they saw a page of Latin, and the only controls were a grey gear and two chevrons. The hour picker opens when you tap the plain title text (`VespersView.swift:199-206`), and *Jump to date* opens from the footer's short date. Neither looks like a control, so the engine's work (any date, three rites, precedence, parallel English) never shows.

**Outcome.** 1.4 makes the app's scope visible:
- **menus you can see**, with a three-way *Menus* setting;
- a proper **Kalendarium**, modelled on Universalis's calendar, which replaces *Jump to date*;
- **native iPad support**, with a two-page spread, and Latin and English on facing pages;
- **faster CI**.

The user approved these on 2026-11-12. They relax CLAUDE.md's "iPhone only" rule, its "no features beyond the brief" rule (the Kalendarium is now in the brief), and the bare chrome.

**The user resubmits** once 1.4 builds. I only supply what they ask for (e.g. a reply draft), and nothing in this plan touches App Store Connect.

## M-CI: Shorter CI (done first, so every later milestone benefits)

**Measured** on main's run of 22 October 2026 (App CI run 196, Oracle audits run 63):

- **App CI: 65 minutes.** Checkout 1, data file 1, XcodeGen under 1, build 1, and the
  **UI tests 61**: about 10 for building for testing and booting the simulator, then the
  classes one after another. In seconds: VespersMatrix 590, Matins 500, DayHours 394,
  Ambrosian 344, LaunchAndNavigation 336, VotiveOffices 229, Theme 211, Dominican 163,
  Font 142, Gallery 105.
- **Oracle audits: 50 minutes** wall time, across 21 jobs. Each job's `swift build` takes
  about 1 minute, so caching `.build` would save little. The time goes to the tests. The
  longest job was *dominican day hours 2025-2028 and 2044* at 48 minutes, because it also
  carried the 2044 hold-out, priest on and off. Next were *dominican matins 2025-2028 and
  2044* at 36 and the other Dominican day-hour jobs at about 33. Two jobs queued for 10-14
  minutes behind the free plan's 20 concurrent Linux jobs.

**Changes**:

1. **App CI builds once and tests on five runners.** `build` runs `build-for-testing`,
   checks the bundle, and passes `Build/Products` on as a tarball. Five `ui-tests` jobs then
   run `test-without-building`, each on its own classes, balanced on the times above (about
   10 minutes of tests each):
   - VespersMatrix;
   - Matins and Gallery;
   - DayHours and Theme;
   - Ambrosian and VotiveOffices;
   - LaunchAndNavigation, Dominican and Font.

   A last job gathers the PNGs into one `snapshots` artifact, with a folder per group. The
   `only_testing` input still works, and each group runs only the tests asked for that
   belong to it. Expected wall time is about 25 minutes. Five is the free plan's macOS
   concurrency, so the iPad job (M3) joins a group rather than adding a sixth.
2. **The Dominican 2044 hold-out is its own audit job**, taken out of the two Dominican
   2025-2028 jobs. To keep within 20 jobs, the Roman *little office* (18 minutes) and
   *office of the dead* (8) become one job. Every check still runs on every push that
   touches the Kit (the 2026-10-03 decision). Expected wall time is about 35 minutes.
3. **Not done**: caching `.build` and the data file, which save about a minute each, and
   regrouping snapshot tests by launch, which stays the next step if a group grows past
   15 minutes.

The timings measured after the change are recorded here once the first run finishes.

## M1: Menus, a three-way setting

The new `Menus` setting is an enum in `SettingsStore` (`@AppStorage`), shown as an English row under *Appearance* in `SettingsView`:

- **Standard** (the default):
  - the navigation row has the gear (left), the hour title as a real `Button` with a small `chevron.down` (centre), and a **calendar** button and the ‹ › day chevrons (right);
  - all of it is drawn in the **theme's rubric colour** (`theme.rubric`, e.g. `#FF8080` on Nox);
  - the footer's short date also opens the Kalendarium.
- **Prayer**: today's look, in the grey chrome colour (`theme.chrome`), with no calendar button or picker chevron. The title still opens the hour picker, and the footer date and date line open the Kalendarium.
- **Hidden**:
  - the navigation row, the TOC icon and the footer's short date are gone;
  - the footer shows only the page number (`3`, not `Page 3 of 12`);
  - page 1 keeps the date line, the day title block and the hour title.

  **Tapping the page number, or the top strip where the navigation row would be**, brings the menus back (drawn as in Prayer). They hide again on the next page turn, or after about 5 seconds.

Files: `App/Breviarium/Vespers/VespersView.swift` (`navigationHeader`, `footer`, the TOC button on page 1, the header in `OfficeReaders.swift` if it draws the TOC icon), `SettingsStore.swift`, `SettingsView.swift`. Keep the accessibility identifiers (`hourPickerButton`, `settingsButton`, …). In Hidden, the tap target gets its own identifier, so the UI tests can reach it.

UI tests: one snapshot per mode on the Nox theme and one on a light theme, plus a test that a tap in Hidden reveals the menus.

## M2: Kalendarium (replacing Jump to date)

**First, the user sends the Universalis calendar screenshot.** I measure it (layout, rows, type sizes, colours and the hanging indent, in pt at @3x) and write the spec as text into CLAUDE.md before building. Until then the layout below is provisional, and the screenshot decides.

**Kit**: a new `Packages/BreviariumKit/Sources/BreviariumKit/Calendar/Kalendarium.swift`.
- `public struct KalendariumEntry { day, month, year, weekday, classis (I–IV, from the engine's rank), nameLine, commemorationLine, color }`.
- `entries(year:month:rite:)`. For Roman and Dominican it is built on `LiturgicalCalendarEngine.day(day:month:year:)` (`LiturgicalDay.swift:98`), with the context from `ConditionalContextBuilder.build(..., ad: "Laudes", ...)`, as `OfficeDataStore.calendarColors` does (`OfficeDataStore.swift:214`). For the Ambrosian rite, use the Ambrosian calendar's `titleBlock` and `color`.
- **No new rubrics or calendar logic.** Every decision (which feast wins, its class, its colour, the commemorations) comes from the existing framework, unchanged: `LiturgicalCalendarEngine`, `Occurrence`, `SanctoralCalendar`, `ConditionalContextBuilder`, and the Ambrosian calendar. `Kalendarium.swift` is only a thin loop over the days of a month, calling those and copying their results into `KalendariumEntry`. It is the same thing `calendarColors` already does for the colours, extended to the title block. It adds no rules of its own, and the view just shows the entries.

**App**:
- A new `App/Breviarium/Vespers/KalendariumView.swift`: a month as a list with **one row per day**, roughly `Thu 12   III   S. Martini Papae et Martyris   ●`. That is the weekday and day number, the class as a Roman numeral (I–IV, or blank where the engine gives none), the day's name from the engine's title block, and the liturgical colour (reuse `CalendarDot`) at the end. Today is marked.
  - Settle the exact spacing, sizes and colours from the screenshot.
  - Settle with the user: whether the commemoration line appears, and how a long name wraps (hanging indent or truncation).
  - The weekday labels come from the screenshot (English or Latin abbreviations).
- Move between months, and tap a day to open that date at the current hour.
- It follows *Ritus* and uses the theme's colours and the chosen font.
- `OfficeDataStore.kalendarium(year:month:rite:)` caches each month, like `colorCache`. Compute off the main actor if a month takes more than about 50 ms.
- On iPad it is shown as a large sheet, or as a wider layout if the screenshot suggests one.

**Remove *Jump to date*.** Delete the `datePickerSheet` and `showingDatePicker` (`VespersView.swift:121`, 300-320), `MonthCalendarView.swift` (keeping `CalendarDot`, which moves to the Kalendarium), and `calendarColors` if nothing else uses it. Every entry point (the calendar button, the date line, the footer date) now opens the Kalendarium. Update the UI tests that used `jumpToDateButton`.

**Tests**:
- Kit `KalendariumTests`. 16 Sep 2026 gives the visual spec's title block. Also: 8 Dec on an Advent Sunday, the Annunciation transferred in 2027, Easter 2038, and one Dominican and one Ambrosian date. Each entry must equal the `titleBlock` that day's Lauds shows.
- UI: a snapshot of the Kalendarium on iPhone and on iPad.

## M3: Native iPad, with a two-page spread

**Project** (`App/project.yml`):
- `TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY: "1,2"`, at lines 19 and 97.
- All four orientations on iPad. iOS 26 ignores `UIRequiresFullScreen`, so the reader must take any window size (Stage Manager, split view). The paginator already reflows to `pageSize`.
- Update the device-family check in `app-ci.yml` (it expects `[1]`) to `[1,2]`.

**The same settings on iPad as on iPhone.** Every setting works on iPad and means the same thing there: *Scrolling* (horizontal pages or vertical scroll), *Page turn* (curl or slide), *Menus*, *Text size*, *Theme*, *Font*, English, *Psalterium*, *Ritus* / *Officium*, the priest toggle, *Rubricæ* and the app icon. The iPad adds no settings of its own, and the spread is just how horizontal pages are laid out on a wide window. With *Scrolling* set to vertical on iPad there is one continuous scroll. With English on it uses the existing side-by-side columns, with the measure capped as below. The settings stay shared through `SettingsStore` (`@AppStorage`).

**Spread** (inside the approved UIKit exception, `OfficeReaders.swift`):
- Use a spread when the window is horizontally regular and wider than tall, with each page at least about 500 pt wide. Otherwise show one page, as on the iPhone.
- **English off**:
  - `paginate()` lays the text out at the width of one page, and pages flow left to right across each spread.
  - Page curl uses `UIPageViewController` with `.spineLocation: .mid` and `isDoubleSided = true`, handing out two `OfficePageController`s per turn. Slide uses a spread controller holding two pages, paged by spread. An odd last page gets a blank partner.
  - The page-1 header is on the left page.
  - The footer reads `Page 3–4 of 12`, or `3–4` in Hidden.
- **English on: facing pages.** The **left page is Latin and the right page is English**, like a facing-page bilingual edition.
  - `ParallelLayout` lays each unit out once, with its Latin column the width of a page and its English column the width of a page. Pages break where both sides fit, so every spread pairs the same units on both sides, aligned unit by unit (verse by verse for the Vulgate, the whole psalm for Pius XII, as now).
  - Each spread counts as one page in the footer (`Page 3 of 9`, or `3` in Hidden), since the left and right are the same text.
  - The Matins lessons are on facing pages too, not stacked.
  - The page-1 header spans the left page. The English side starts at the same height, so the units stay level.
- **iPad portrait, and narrow windows**: one page, with today's side-by-side columns when English is on.
- Margins scale with the page, with the line length capped at about 34 em on a full-width portrait page.
- Sheets (Settings, Horæ, Kalendarium, TOC) are form sheets. Check each one. The TOC jumps to the spread that holds the section.

**CI and listing**:
- An **iPad Air 11" (M3)** job in the M-CI matrix covers:
  - Vespers page 1 and a psalmody spread, in landscape with English off and on;
  - portrait with English off and on;
  - Matins lessons on facing pages;
  - the Kalendarium;
  - Menus = Hidden;
  - vertical *Scrolling*, and the *Page turn* slide, so iPad's settings match the iPhone's.
- `promo.yml`, `PromoUITests.swift` and `scripts/promo/compose.py`: iPad 13" screenshots at 2064×2752, which App Store Connect requires once iPad is supported. They show the spread, facing-page English, the Kalendarium and the Standard menus. Add a Kalendarium still to the iPhone set too.
