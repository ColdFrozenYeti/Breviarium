# 1.5: widgets

**Approved 14 November 2026.** 1.5 adds Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets, so the App
Store build is more app-like after the 4.2 rejection that 1.4 answers. A widget shows
what the app already shows: the day, its colour, the hour for the time of day and that
hour's antiphon. Tapping a widget opens that hour. It follows 1.4 (native iPad).

## 1. Decisions (14 November 2026)

1. **Widgets are in the brief** (`CLAUDE.md`, *No features beyond the brief*). A widget
   is a view onto the office the app already computes. It adds no tracking, checkmarks,
   reminders, notifications, sync or audio. *No explanatory text* holds: a widget shows
   only liturgical text, the date line, the day's title and the hour's title. There is no
   "Tap to pray" and no English label.
2. **The App Store and TestFlight build carries the widget; the sideloaded build doesn't.**
   The rule *No capability a free Apple ID can't sign* is rewritten (`CLAUDE.md`).
   - The extension is `com.epavone.breviarium.widget`, with no App Group and no other
     capability.
   - `testflight.yml` signs it as it signs the app: automatic signing with the App
     Store Connect key creates its App ID and profile on the first run.
   - `build-ipa.yml` deletes `Payload/Breviarium.app/PlugIns/` before zipping. A free
     Apple ID never signs an extension, the App ID count stays as it is, and the
     sideloaded app works as before, without widgets.
3. **No App Group, so the widget has its own settings.** The extension can't read the
   app's `UserDefaults`, so each widget is configured from *Edit Widget* (an App Intents
   `WidgetConfigurationIntent`, stored by the system, no entitlement). The options are
   *Ritus*, *Officium*, *Psalterium*, *Theme* and *Font*, defaulting to *Romanus*,
   *Officium diei*, the Vulgate, *Nox* and Hoefler Text. *Psalterium* was added
   2026-11-14, since the psalmody's antiphons follow the psalter; it is disabled under
   *Ambrosianus*, as in the app. A change in the app's Settings doesn't reach a widget already
   placed; you edit the widget too.
4. **The widgets** (the user's choice):
   - **Day**, small and medium. Small: the colour dot, the class and the day's name.
     Medium: also the date line and the commemoration line. No hour's title: a tap
     opens the hour anyway (decided 2026-11-14, after the first snapshots).
   - **Hour**, medium and large: the hour's title, then its antiphon (§3), centred in
     the room under the title. Large also has the date line, the class, the day's name
     and the page's rule. Where the hour has no antiphon, the day's name is the text,
     and isn't shown twice.
   - **Lock Screen**: rectangular (the hour's title, the day's name, the class), inline
     (the hour's title) and circular. The circular one is the app's logo in a ring, the
     same at every hour (decided 2026-11-14): the default icon's *B*, traced to a vector
     by `scripts/widget-logo.py`, drawn as a template so the system tints it.
   - On iPad the same Home Screen sizes, small, medium and large, and the Lock Screen
     ones. No extra-large.
5. **Tapping a widget** opens its hour and date in the reader:
   `breviarium://open?hour=<Vespera>&date=2026-11-14`, a `CFBundleURLTypes` scheme (not
   an associated domain, and not network). It takes the same path as a tap on a
   Kalendarium day, with the app's own *Ritus* and *Officium*. Saved settings never
   change.
6. **The timeline**: a day per timeline, with an entry at midnight and at each change
   of `CanonicalHour.forTimeOfDay` (05, 09, 12, 15, 17, 20). It reloads after midnight.
   - Under the Little Office and the Office of the Dead the hour goes through
     `Officium.availableHour(for:)`, as in the app.
   - Under *Ambrosianus* it is Compline all day.
7. **Fonts**: the widget registers the app's bundled OFL fonts from the app's own
   bundle (`CTFontManagerRegisterFontsForURL`). If the extension can't read them there,
   it carries its own copies (about 1 MB).

## 2. The data: memory and time (measured 14 November 2026)

Main's data file is **14.78 MB** of JSON (English 5.85 MB, Latin 8.91, the Pius XII
psalter 0.28, Ambrosian 0.05, calendars and tables under 0.02). The app decodes all of
it at launch. WidgetKit holds an extension to about 30 MB. That figure is the usual
one; Apple doesn't document it, and the simulator doesn't enforce it.

**Decoding the whole file is too much for a widget.** On Linux (Swift 6.3.3, release),
decoding the Latin-only file added 37 MB at peak to a 16.7 MB baseline, and 21 MB that
stayed. The whole file added 55 MB.

**A day reads very little of it.** Every hour of a day, under each rite and office,
read at most **80 of the corpus's 3,873 files** (0.48 MB of JSON; 16 September 2026
under *Dominicanus* is the largest, and 14 November 2026 under *Romanus* reads 48).

**So the widget has its own data file**, `breviarium-widget.pack`:
- `BreviariumData` writes it next to the app's file, from the same `DataBundle`, without
  the English: **9.18 MB**.
- The pack holds a small header (217 KB: the calendars, the transfer tables, the
  Ambrosian data and an index), then each file of the corpus as its own JSON.
- `PackedBundle` reads it memory-mapped and decodes a file only when the engine first
  asks for it.
- It is still the whole corpus, so any date works. Nothing is precomputed, and there is
  no ordo.
- It sits in the app's bundle. The widget reads it from there, and so does the app's
  widget gallery (§4).

**Measured** (`BreviariumData --widget-budget`, run by every `BreviariumData` run):
- Reading the header takes **0.02 s**. A day's timeline (7 entries) takes **0.33 s** in
  all on Linux.
- It adds **2.8 MB** of dirty memory. On Linux that's the anonymous resident set,
  because iOS doesn't charge a process for clean, file-backed pages (the mapped pack,
  the libraries' code).
- The budget is 10 MB and 2 s; the run fails if a day goes over.

## 3. The antiphon (decided 14 November 2026)

- **Lauds, Vespers, Compline**: the Gospel canticle's antiphon (*Benedictus*,
  *Magnificat*, *Nunc dimittis*), as said after the canticle. That is the last antiphon
  of the `canticum` section: the whole antiphon in every rite, where the one before the
  canticle can be an intonation.
  - **Ambrosian Compline**: the source gives *Salva nos.* before the *Nunc dimittis* and
    the whole antiphon after it. The widget shows the whole one, from the 1957 breviary
    as `AmbrosianCompline` assembles it, with no Roman text.
- **Matins**: the first nocturn's first antiphon (`nocturnusI` or `adNocturnum`), not
  the invitatory.
- **Prime, Terce, Sext, None**: the psalmody's first antiphon.
- **The day's Latin title** when the section has no antiphon (decided 2026-11-14): the
  name line of the title block, set as the title is. At the little hours of Good Friday
  that is *Feria Sexta in Passione et Morte Domini*. No other antiphon stands in
  (`WidgetDay.hourText`).
- **Latin only**, as the page prints it, following the widget's *Psalterium*.
- **As large as fits, never crammed** (decided 2026-11-14). The day's name and the
  hour's text take the largest of their sizes that fits whole, centred in the room they
  have. Only when even the smallest doesn't fit does the text end at a word with an
  ellipsis (`…`) at its line limit. Nothing is shrunk below the smallest size
  (`WidgetType` in `App/Shared/WidgetFace.swift`):

  | Face | Text | Sizes, points | Lines at the smallest |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Day, small | the day's name | 30 to 17 | 4 |
  | Day, medium | the name (the commemoration at 14) | 30 to 18 | 2 (2) |
  | Hour, medium | the antiphon or the day's title | 22 to 15 | 5 |
  | Hour, large | the antiphon or the day's title | 28 to 16 | 10 |
  | Lock Screen, rectangular | the day's name | 14 | 1 |
  | Lock Screen, inline | the hour's title | the system's | 1 |

  The hour's title, the class and the date line are never cut. The first proposal was
  fixed sizes with 3, 2 (1) and 3 lines; the first snapshots showed the room unused, and
  the user asked for larger text, centred, on 14 November 2026.

## 4. Testing without a Mac

CI can't reliably place a widget on a simulator's Home Screen; that would mean scripting
SpringBoard. Instead:
- The widget's views live in a shared folder compiled into both the app and the
  extension.
- A launch argument, `-BREVIARIUM_WIDGET_GALLERY`, opens a debug-only screen in the app.
  It draws every widget at that device's exact sizes, in full colour and in an imitation
  of the tinted and Lock Screen looks, from the same pack the widget reads.
- `WidgetGalleryUITests` captures it for fixed dates and themes on the iPhone 17 (the
  `launch-theme` group) and on the iPad Air 11" (M3), next to `IPadUITests`.
- The PNGs join the `snapshots` artifact and are measured like the office's pages.
- Only the phone shows the system's own tinting, the clear Liquid Glass look, the Lock
  Screen's vibrancy and a real tap: the user checks those on TestFlight once per release.

## 6. Questions, settled (14 November 2026)

1. **A *Psalterium* option?** Yes (§1.3). With the Pius XII psalter, every fortnight
   of 2026, every Roman hour's antiphon is on DO's Pius XII page
   (`widgetPiusXIIAntiphonsAreOnDivinumOfficiumsPiusXIIPages`).
2. **An hour without an antiphon** shows the day's Latin title (§3).
3. **Long texts** are cut off at a line limit with an ellipsis, not shrunk (§3).
